SDR SDR playbook

Mission: generate qualified pipeline. You own two lanes — inbound (accept, validate, and convert demo requests fast) and outbound (prospect target accounts into booked meetings). You are credited for every downstream outcome (SAL, SQL, SQO, Won, ARR) on the logos you originate, via the Orig-SDR trace.

A day in the life

  1. Start in Outreach. Clear your task queue — calls first, then emails. Follow Day-to-Day Management in Outreach.
  2. Work inbound immediately as it arrives. Accept in CRM, validate, call within 5 minutes. Full process in Inbound lead expectations. New MQLs go into sequence: Adding an MQL to Sequence.
  3. Prospect target accounts. Prioritize ICP tier and segment (see ICP & targeting). SMB-Large + Vagaro displacement is the highest-yield outbound motion. Bulk-add from account views: Account Views to Bulk Add to Sequence.
  4. Personalize with the persona and pain. Open with a pain in the buyer's words (see Personas and the pain library under Core / MedSpa). Name the incumbent software in the second line, never the first.
  5. Book it live. When you reach a decision-maker, book the intro call or demo on the spot, confirm verbally and in writing, send the calendar invite, and add to the confirmation sequence.
  6. Log everything in Salesforce. Create the Account, Contact (with role), and Opportunity correctly — see Salesforce processes. Notes should capture reason for interest, key pains, timeline, and current software.
  7. Hand off cleanly. Confirm the AE owner, make sure the opp stage and notes are correct, and remove the prospect from the prospecting sequence.

Qualification: what a good meeting looks like

  • Business is legitimate (name, website, or Google listing) and an ICP business type — Salon, Spa, Salon & Spa, MedSpa, School.
  • Segment confirmed: providers and locations. Remember Solo is MedSpa-only.
  • Decision-maker or strong influencer on the call (owner, multi-location operator, GM/practice manager, medical director).
  • Current software identified plus one or two meaningful business challenges (KPIs, efficiency, growth, compliance).
  • Not an Exclude sub-type (Resort/Hotel Spa, Plastic Surgery, Hair Replacement, Other > Other) and not a hard disqualifier (see ICP).

Mindset: information alone doesn't close deals — conversations do. Use the Inbound Qualification Training & Talk Tracks with a Challenger mindset.

The deal motion

  1. Prep. Before every call, review the SDR's notes, the persona (who you're talking to), segment, current software, and the matching Crayon battlecard. Pull the Discovery Questions.
  2. Discovery. Uncover reporting gaps, client-experience issues, operational inefficiencies, and (MedSpa) charting, inventory and compliance needs. Get the prospect deep enough to raise objections — logos that raise functionality, onboarding and pricing questions convert 2–3.5x better than silent ones. Stamp the Moved to Discovery date.
  3. Demo. Show the workflow that solves the pains they named, not a feature tour. Lead with reporting — it gets a positive reaction in 52% of demos and is a top win driver (hero reports: MA200, Growth Indicators, Goals Dashboard, DE40). Keep MedSpa evaluations anchored in front-of-house productivity (scheduling, checkout, memberships) — our win rate drops when the buyer fixates on EMR depth.
  4. Multi-thread. For SMB-Large and Mid-Market, include the GM/practice manager and remember the finance gatekeeper who won't be on the demo — arm your champion with cost/ROI.
  5. Quote. Build the quote in Salesforce (recall, send, discounts — see Salesforce processes). Run the MeevoPay rate calculator on every deal. Stamp Moved to Negotiate.
  6. Close. Confirm attendees, agenda and outcomes for every next step; every open account gets a touch at least every 3 business days.
  7. Handoff. Client-facing conversion checklists and data review tickets go to the SE team (Ryan Burns). See Integrations & implementation.

Demo principles that correlate with wins

  • Reporting and ease of use are the validated win drivers (Clozd) — lead with them. Price is the loss lever — neutralize it early; don't lead on discount.
  • Quality of support, responsiveness and follow-up are the most-mentioned positive drivers in Clozd interviews — your follow-up cadence is part of the product.
  • Interface modernity and pricing transparency skew negative in interviews — address UI head-on with speed and role-specific views, and be clear and simple on pricing.
  • Owner-operators respond to hours saved per week and take-home dollars, not "efficiency" or "enterprise-grade" anything. Multi-location operators want live multi-location reporting, not slideware.

The SE motion

  1. Teach first. Every new rep goes through your Product Training modules in weeks 1 to 3 (New Hire Sales Onboarding), and the team gets a product or MeevoIQ session on the live calendar. Record every session and attach the deck so it lands in the Session Library.
  2. Scope before you demo. Take the AE's discovery notes and the persona. Agree what the technical demo needs to prove and what it should not wander into. A technical demo that turns into a feature tour costs us the deal as surely as an AE demo does, and in MedSpa the EMR rabbit-hole is the classic loss pattern.
  3. Prove it, don't describe it. Show the actual workflow on the prospect's scenario: their service menu, their locations, their integration. Quantify the outcome the same way the AE does (Business Impact Positioning).
  4. Own the data review. Client-facing conversion checklists and data review tickets come to you. Review the source system export, flag what will not migrate cleanly, and give the AE a plain-English summary they can put in front of the prospect before the quote. See Integrations, implementation & conversions.
  5. Roadmap discipline. You will be asked "can it do X?" more than anyone. If it is live, show it. If it is roadmap, say roadmap and commit to no date. If it is not planned, say so and move to how the client achieves the outcome today. Never invent a workaround you have not tested.
  6. Hand off cleanly. Complete the implementation handoff checklist with the AE: scope sold, integrations, data sources, hardware, go-live expectation, named client contact. Join the kickoff. What gets lost here becomes a support ticket in month two.
  7. Feed it back. Every "can it do X?" you could not answer with a yes goes to the product questions channel and, if it recurs, to Enablement for the release notes and battlecards.

  1. Open with their numbers, not ours. Every review starts from the client's own data: revenue, retention, rebooking, no-shows, processing volume, product sales. A gap in their numbers is the only honest reason to introduce a feature. If you cannot point at a number, you are not ready to pitch.

SDR Inbound lead expectations

Inbound = a demo-request MQL (GetDemo / Demo Form). Speed and follow-through are everything. There are two paths depending on whether the prospect booked a call.

Part 1 — Intro call booked (15-minute intro call)

  1. Lead acceptance & validation (within 5 minutes). Accept the lead immediately in CRM. Verify the business is legitimate (business name, website or Google listing), an industry fit (salon, spa, medspa), and a decision-maker or strong influencer. If legitimate: convert Lead → Account + Contact and confirm correct ownership (AE assigned). Outcome: the lead is now a Sales Accepted Lead (SAL).
  2. Immediate outreach (within 5 minutes of acceptance). Call first. If no answer, leave a short voicemail and send a confirmation email plus calendar confirmation. Goals: confirm the intro call time, try to move it sooner if availability exists, set expectations for the call.
  3. Intro call execution (15 minutes). Use the Inbound Qualification Script with a Challenger mindset. Confirm business type, size and role; identify current software and high-level dissatisfaction or growth friction; uncover 1–2 meaningful business challenges (KPIs, efficiency, growth, compliance); validate fit. If time allows, treat it as light discovery: reporting gaps, client-experience issues, operational inefficiencies, MedSpa-specific needs (charting, inventory, compliance).
  4. Control the close: book the demo live on the call. Immediately after: send the calendar invite and add the prospect to the Demo Confirmation Sequence. Confirm date/time, attendees, agenda and outcomes. CRM: Stage → Demo Scheduled; notes updated with pains and goals.

Part 2 — Prospect requests more information (no call booked)

  1. Lead acceptance & validation (within 5 minutes). Accept immediately; verify business type/fit and web presence. If legitimate, convert Lead → Account + Contact. Outcome: SAL.
  2. Add to the Inbound Demo Request Sequence. This ensures structured multi-touch follow-up, consistent messaging and task accountability.
  3. Immediate call attempt. Call right after enrolling. If no answer, leave a voicemail aligned to their request. Objective: shift from passive interest to a scheduled conversation.
  4. Follow the sequence and complete every task — calls and emails. Personalize with their business type, likely KPIs (growth, retention, productivity) and common switching triggers.
  5. Book the intro call or demo on the spot once live contact is made; confirm next steps verbally and in writing. Then remove the prospect from the inbound sequence, send the calendar invite, and add to the appropriate confirmation sequence. CRM: stage updated (Intro Scheduled or Demo Scheduled); notes reflect reason for interest, key pains and timeline indicators.
What counts as inbound in reporting: a logo is Inbound only when it is linked to a demo-request MQL (the opp is created within 180 days of the MQL date). Everything else — including scored/nurture MQLs and all outbound — reports in the Outbound lane. Marketing owns MQL:SAL on the inbound lane; the outbound funnel starts at SAL.

Rules worth remembering

  • Two lanes. Inbound (Hot Fill Form) = linked to a demo-request MQL within 180 days. Outbound (Target Account) = every other SAL. The Salesforce Inbound/Outbound picklist is not used.
  • Two ARR lenses. Total ARR = Software + MeevoPay + MeevoTips (board reporting). Software ARR = software only (quota attainment and comp). Always say which one you mean.

Controllable DQs (Non-Responsive, Not Interested, Budget, Timing, Lost to Competitor) stay in the denominator.

Discovery principles

  • Confirm business type, size and role before anything else — it decides the persona, the segment and whether the account is ICP.
  • Ask about current software and contract renewal date every time.
  • Get to one or two measurable business challenges (a KPI, a cost, a compliance risk) — not a feature wish list.
  • Invite objections. Functionality, onboarding and pricing questions are signs of a real evaluation.
  • Terminology: say "data migration limitations", not "data migration issues"; say "injectable tracking", not "Product Usage Tracking"; say "HIPAA-aware" unless compliance is verified for the specific claim.

            FAQ

            Is a Solo (one-provider) business worth pursuing?
            Only if it's a MedSpa. Solo is MedSpa-only ICP; every other Solo is a hard DQ.
            What counts as a SAL / SQL / SQO?
            SAL = first meeting set (opp created, Suspect). SQL = first meeting held (Moved to Discovery date). SQO = demo conducted (Moved to Negotiate date). See KPI definitions.
            The prospect says they just renewed with a competitor. Now what?
            Record the renewal date, spend and satisfaction in CRM, park for 9 months, and re-engage 90 days before the next renewal. See renewal timing under SDR playbook.
            A demo no-show hasn't rescheduled. When do I close it?
            If the opp is still in Suspect 14 days after the no-show, mark it Closed Lost. Keep a touch every 3 business days until then.
            How do I find the battlecard for a competitor?
            Crayon — via the Competition tab in Salesforce or the links under Competitors.
            A resort spa or plastic surgery practice filled out a demo form. Do I book it?
            No — both are Exclude sub-types (PMS or EMR/EHR requirements Meevo doesn't support). Disqualify with the appropriate reason.
            Which ARR number is "my" number?
            Quota attainment uses Software ARR. Total ARR (Software + MeevoPay + MeevoTips) is what the business reports. Always say which lens you mean.
            Can I say Meevo is HIPAA compliant?
            Use "HIPAA-aware" language for general claims and check the specific capability (secure photo storage, controlled permissions, audit trails) against the MedSpa feature notes before making a compliance claim. Escalate specifics to the SE team.
            Where is pricing?
            The Marketing share doc holds pricing, features and client-facing guides; MeevoPay rates come from the rate calculators. See Pricing.
            Who do I ask about a MedSpa deal vs a Core deal?
            MedSpa → Melanie Hull. Core → Taylor Meeker (AE) or Nicole Fabre (multi-location). Enterprise → Nicole Fabre. See Who do I ask?
